# Casphe SEO And Positioning Plan

## Goal

Launch Casphe with a clear acquisition strategy that matches the product's actual strength:

- not a generic cafe discovery app
- not a broad "best places to work" media site
- a data-driven product to find cafes with fast Wi-Fi for work

The launch priority is:

- language: English
- acquisition: localized SEO
- initial markets: Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City

## Core Positioning

### Main promise

`Find cafes with the fastest Wi-Fi for work.`

### Secondary promise

`Discover laptop-friendly cafes with real speed test data.`

### What Casphe is

- a local discovery tool for remote workers, freelancers, students, and digital nomads
- a map + ranking experience based on measured internet performance
- a product with objective proof, not just subjective recommendations

### What Casphe is not

- not a general "best cafes" guide
- not a lifestyle blog
- not a broad review platform about coffee quality, decor, or food

## Strategic Choice

### Why local SEO first

Local SEO is the most logical strategy because the user intent is inherently geographic.

People do not usually search for cafes with good Wi-Fi in the abstract. They search for:

- a cafe near them
- a cafe in a specific city
- a place where they can work today
- a cafe with reliable and fast internet nearby

This means the strongest search intent is local, even when the wording looks international.

### Why not broad international SEO first

A broad international strategy would be too wide for the current product stage.

Reasons:

- the app is local by nature
- the differentiator is strongest at the city level
- current product data is better suited to city landing pages than to a global editorial strategy
- ranking for broad international work-cafe terms would be much more competitive and less defensible

The right approach is:

`international language, local acquisition`

## Acquisition Audiences

There are two valid acquisition audiences, but they should not have the same weight.

### Primary audience

People explicitly looking for connectivity:

- fast Wi-Fi cafe
- best Wi-Fi cafe in Hanoi
- cafe with good internet in Ho Chi Minh City
- where to work with fast Wi-Fi

This audience is the strongest fit because Casphe has a real product advantage here: measured speed data.

### Secondary audience

People looking for a place to work:

- best cafes to work from in Hanoi
- laptop-friendly cafes in Ho Chi Minh City
- quiet cafes to work in Hanoi

This audience matters, but it is broader and more subjective. Casphe should address it without making it the main promise.

### Positioning rule

Always connect "place to work" with "measured Wi-Fi quality".

Do not position Casphe as a generic productivity cafe guide unless the product later collects richer workplace data such as:

- power outlets
- noise level
- seating comfort
- opening hours fit for work
- meeting friendliness

## Messaging

### Recommended headline territory

- `Find cafes with the fastest Wi-Fi for work in Hanoi`
- `Best measured cafe Wi-Fi in Ho Chi Minh City`
- `Compare cafe internet speeds before you go`

### Messaging principles

- lead with measurable performance
- keep the tone practical, not lifestyle-heavy
- use "for work" as a use case, not as an unproven ranking claim
- prefer "best Wi-Fi for work" over "best cafe to work from"

### Avoid

- "Top 10 best cafes" without qualification
- subjective claims not supported by product data
- overemphasis on coffee culture if the product does not measure it

## Information Architecture

### Public structure

- `/`
- `/cities/hanoi`
- `/cities/ho-chi-minh-city`

### Homepage role

The homepage should:

- explain the product clearly in English
- show the map experience
- link users into the city pages
- support the product-level query space

### City page role

Each city page should be the main SEO landing page for local search intent.

It should include:

- clear H1 in English
- short explanatory intro
- map centered on the city
- top 10 cafes with the best Wi-Fi for work
- city-level stats
- methodology section

### Why city pages are better than cafe pages for now

Individual cafe pages are not the best launch priority yet.

Reasons:

- likely not enough data depth per cafe
- higher risk of thin pages
- more maintenance for weaker SEO value
- city pages let Casphe aggregate multiple useful signals into one stronger landing page

For now, cafe-level information should live inside city pages.

## Ranking Philosophy

The ranking should not pretend to identify the "best cafes" overall.

It should rank:

`cafes with the best measured Wi-Fi for work`

### Ranking inputs

- average download speed
- average upload speed
- average latency
- number of tests
- last tested recency as a freshness signal if needed

### Ranking safeguards

- cafes without tests should not enter the main Top 10
- cafes with too few tests should be labeled `Limited data`
- if a city has fewer than 10 tested cafes, show fewer than 10 instead of padding weak entries

## Editorial Angle Per City

### Hanoi

Target query families:

- best wifi cafe hanoi
- best cafes to work from in hanoi
- fast wifi cafe hanoi
- laptop friendly cafe hanoi

### Ho Chi Minh City

Target query families:

- best wifi cafe ho chi minh city
- best cafes to work from in ho chi minh city
- fast wifi cafe district 1
- laptop friendly cafe saigon

Note:

Search behavior may mix `Ho Chi Minh City` and `Saigon`, so the wording can acknowledge both in copy and metadata where relevant.

## SEO Content Principles

### What should be indexable

- product explanation on the homepage
- city summaries
- top tested cafes lists
- methodology copy

### What should not be the core SEO surface

- raw map interface alone
- API responses
- pages with too little content or too little data

### Content style

- concise
- factual
- useful
- data-backed

## Brand Angle

Casphe should feel:

- practical
- credible
- local
- useful for real work situations

It should not feel like:

- a vague startup directory
- a generic cafe review app
- a blog making subjective recommendations without evidence

## Summary

The recommended launch strategy is:

1. Use English publicly.
2. Focus on localized SEO, not broad international SEO.
3. Launch with Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City city pages.
4. Keep the map experience, but support it with indexable city content.
5. Position Casphe around measured Wi-Fi quality for work.
6. Treat "best cafes to work from" as a secondary angle, not the core claim.

In one sentence:

`Casphe should win by becoming the most useful local resource for finding cafes with fast, measured Wi-Fi for work.`
